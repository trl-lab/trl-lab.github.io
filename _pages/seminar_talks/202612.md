Iñigo Alonso is a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Edinburgh, working with Prof. Mirella Lapata on how vision-language models reason over real-world documents that combine text, tables, figures and images. Tables have been his long-standing focus, but his work now spans documents as a whole, with a particular interest in making visual reasoning more efficient. He received his PhD from the University of the Basque Country, supervised by Prof. Eneko Agirre.

<b>"Efficient Representations for Visually Rich Document Tables"</b>
<br>

<p>
<b>Abstract</b>: Tables are among the most challenging document elements for current large language models to interpret, yet most approaches still represent them as serialised text: a lossy, flat sequence that obscures their two-dimensional structure and discards the visual information they often carry. In this talk, I will discuss how approaches have evolved from treating tables as strictly structured text to treating them as heterogeneous, multimodal elements embedded in documents alongside text, figures and other tables. 

I will begin with the direct benefits of representing tables as images (PixT3). Then, with TABLET, a dataset of 4M examples built from real-world table images, we show that synthetic renderings miss much of what tables in the wild contain. In A Table Is Worth 64 Tokens, we turn to the efficiency benefits of image representations in long, multi-table documents. We show that simply downscaling table images is not enough, and propose an approach that keeps the efficiency gains without sacrificing performance. I will then discuss how these benefits can be carried over from pixel space to embedding space. Finally, I will discuss the current challenges in the area of visually rich document understanding.
</p>
