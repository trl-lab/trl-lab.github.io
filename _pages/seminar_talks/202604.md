<b>"Bringing external knowledge to tabular learning"</b>
<br>

<p>
Tabular data is the dominant format for storing structured information across organizations and underpins many predictive tasks. Yet most tabular learning methods treat string entries as isolated symbols, ignoring the real-world entities they represent. In this talk, we explore different methods for grounding tabular data in external world knowledge to improve predictive performance.
First, we leverage large-scale general-purpose knowledge graphs as an explicit source of structured knowledge. We introduce SEPAL, a scalable embedding approach for efficient knowledge extraction from massive knowledge graphs. SEPAL combines an initial core-embedding step with a relation-aware message-passing algorithm, producing high-quality entity representations for downstream tabular learning.
Second, we explore large language models (LLMs) as an alternative knowledge source. Because LLMs operate directly on raw text, they naturally bypass the entity-matching bottleneck that limits knowledge-graph approaches. To effectively harness this capability, we propose CAMPS (Column-Aware Mean Pooling after Serialization), a strategy that extracts context-aware representations from intermediate LLM layers after row serialization. Our results show that leveraging the implicit knowledge encoded in LLMs currently stands as the most robust and effective method for enriching tabular models and grounding their entries in real-world knowledge.
</p>
