<br>
<b>"Understanding Out-of-Distribution Generalization in Tabular In-Context Learning"</b>
<br>

<p>
Abstract: Tabular foundation models perform in-context learning: at inference they receive labeled example rows from a table (one never seen during pre-training) and predict labels for query rows without any weight updates. Strikingly, such models generalize out of distribution even after self-supervised pre-training on a single real table, a phenomenon that prior coverage, domain similarity, and corpus scale all fail to explain. This talk develops a task-centric account: because pre-training samples a target column and a feature subset, a table is best understood as a generator of feature–target tasks, so what governs transfer is the tasks it induces rather than its row count or semantic domain. We use this view to explain why feature richness matters more than instance count and to derive practical recommendations for building pre-training corpora. Finally, we present evidence that these models generalize by learning to retrieve and aggregate labels from relevant context rows not just through sufficient distributional coverage seen during pre-training.
</p>
